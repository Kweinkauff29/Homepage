Building a Bridge to Brazil: Why Southwest Florida's Moment Is Now

Coconut Coast Organization of REALTORS® · Member Blog
November 16, 2026

A New Partnership, Signed in New Orleans

During the 2026 NAR NXT, The REALTOR® Experience in New Orleans (November 6–8), the Coconut Coast Organization of REALTORS® CEO, Meighan Harris and President, Jeanne Drath, signed a Memorandum of Understanding with CIMI360 Brazil and its CEO, Heitor Kuser. For our members, this agreement is more than a handshake at a conference. It is a formal bridge between Southwest Florida and one of the most loyal, fastest-growing sources of international buyers in our state.

CIMI360, the International Real Estate Market Congress (Congresso Internacional do Mercado Imobiliário), has quickly become one of the largest real estate gatherings in Latin America. Its 2026 edition in Rio de Janeiro drew about 8,600 registered participants and ran alongside the 11th International Congress of CILA, the Latin American Real Estate Confederation. The prior edition in São Paulo brought together developers, hotel groups and investors from more than 20 countries. The next edition is headed to Brasília.

Our goal is simple: help Brazilian buyers, investors and real estate professionals discover the west coast of Florida, and help our members be ready when they arrive.

The Big Picture: Real Estate Is the World's Largest Store of Wealth

To understand why international buyers matter, start with the global numbers:

  • $393.3 trillion. That is the total value of the world's real estate (residential, commercial and agricultural land) at the start of 2025, according to Savills. It is the largest store of wealth on the planet, worth roughly four times global GDP and more than global stocks, bonds and gold combined.
  • 20.7%. The United States' share of the world's total real estate value, second only to China.
  • $45.3 billion. What foreign buyers spent on U.S. existing homes between April 2025 and March 2026, purchasing about 67,100 properties, according to NAR's 2026 International Transactions report.
  • #1. Florida's rank among U.S. states for foreign buyers, attracting 20% of all international purchases nationwide.

International capital moves toward stability, lifestyle and long-term value. Florida offers all three, and the global data confirms that buyers from around the world keep choosing us first.

A few takeaways for our members:

  • Brazilians love Florida, and the trend is climbing. Visit Florida data shows Brazilian visitation growing every year since 2021: from about 132,000 visitors in 2021 to 709,000 in 2022, 1.02 million in 2023, 1.18 million in 2024 and 1.30 million in 2025. More than half of Brazilian visitors to the U.S. include Florida in their trip.
  • Visitors become buyers. According to Florida Realtors®' 2026 international report, 91% of international buyers visited Florida at least once before purchasing. Every Brazilian family that vacations here is a potential future client.
  • There is real buying power. Brazil added more than 9,200 new dollar millionaires in 2025, according to the UBS Global Wealth Report 2026, and has the largest millionaire population in Latin America.

What International Buyers in Florida Look Like

Florida Realtors®' 2026 Profile of International Residential Transactions in Florida gives a useful snapshot of the clients our members can expect:

  • About 13,400 homes purchased by international buyers statewide, totaling $9.0 billion.
  • 61% paid all cash, compared with 48% of international buyers nationwide, which means smoother, faster closings.
  • Median purchase price of $424,000, above the $390,000 median for all Florida existing homes.
  • 58% bought single-family homes.
  • 69% planned to use the property as a vacation home, a rental, or both.
  • 64% came through personal contacts, referrals or former clients. Relationships drive this business, which is exactly what our CIMI360 partnership is designed to build.

Southwest Florida's Opportunity

Today, nearly half of Florida's international buyers (47%) purchase in the Miami–Fort Lauderdale–West Palm Beach area, and Orlando draws another 12%. The west coast already has a meaningful foothold: the Cape Coral–Fort Myers metro attracted 6% of the state's international buyers, Naples–Immokalee–Marco Island 3%, and North Port–Sarasota–Bradenton another 6%.

That is a strong foundation, and plenty of room to grow. Southwest Florida offers what Brazilian buyers are seeking: Gulf Coast beaches and sunsets, golf and boating, a relaxed pace of life, excellent value compared with the state's most crowded markets, and strong potential for vacation and rental use. Many Brazilian families already know Orlando and Miami. Our job is to introduce them to the rest of Florida's story.

And we are not alone. Other Florida associations, including our neighbors in Sarasota and Manatee, have also formed partnerships with CIMI360, a sign that Florida's west coast is building real momentum in the Brazilian market.

How Members Can Get Ready

Here are practical ways to position yourself for Brazilian clients:

  • Speak to the buyer. Portuguese is Brazil's language, not Spanish. Portuguese-language listing descriptions, video tours and social posts make a strong first impression. If you are not a Portuguese speaker, consider building a referral relationship with a colleague who is.
  • Lead with lifestyle and investment. Most international buyers plan vacation or rental use. Be ready to discuss rental rules, HOA and condo policies, property management options, and typical carrying costs, including insurance.
  • Build your global credentials. NAR's Certified International Property Specialist (CIPS) designation is an excellent way to learn cross-border best practices.
  • Know your partners. International transactions involve FIRPTA withholding, currency transfers and tax considerations. Have trusted attorneys, CPAs, lenders and closing agents with international experience on your team.
  • Nurture relationships, not just leads. With most international buyers coming through referrals and personal contacts, staying connected with Brazilian professionals through events like CIMI360 can pay off for years.
  • Welcome visitors. Brazilian families are already traveling to Florida in record numbers. Showing them Southwest Florida during a visit is often the first step toward a purchase.
