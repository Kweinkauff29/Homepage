The Quick Version

LFRO IN THREE POINTS

Core operational guardrails every broker and office manager should know when structuring referral licensees and administrative staff.

01

REFERRALS

The only real estate activity authorized under the waiver. Licensees may send client referrals and receive referral fees through their supervising broker.

02

NO MLS ACCESS

NAR's LFRO policy specifically establishes that LFRO licensees are ineligible for MLS subscriptions, credentials, or system access.

03

SEPARATE ENTITY

The waiver structure requires licensees to be held through a separate legal referral entity, isolated from the REALTOR® firm's brokerage practice.

NAR POLICY GUIDANCE • FAQ #1

"WHAT ABOUT JUST ONE FAMILY TRANSACTION?"

NAR specifically addresses this common broker question: The LFRO waiver does not permit a licensee to list a property for a family member or friend as an exception. To list or sell any property, the licensee must be placed with an active REALTOR® brokerage firm and hold proper MLS subscriber access.

Regulatory Guidance

ADMINISTRATIVE SUPPORT WITHOUT CROSSING THE LICENSING LINE

The Florida Real Estate Commission (FREC) and DBPR provide official guidance defining permissible administrative duties for support staff under broker supervision.

  • Fill out and submit listing agreements and status changes to the MLS under direct broker supervision.
  • Provide objective, written property information strictly from materials prepared and approved by the broker.
  • Place approved listing signs, lockboxes, and marketing flyers at listed properties.
  • Assemble closing files, track required disclosures, and coordinate document delivery to title and lending entities.
  • Follow up on loan commitments, appraisal schedules, and title status under broker oversight.
  • Schedule appointments for appraisers, home inspectors, pest inspectors, and closing agents.
  • Coordinate open house dates, prepare printed packets, and staff registration tables (without discussing terms or showing property independently).
  • Draft promotional materials, advertisements, and social media announcements for mandatory broker review and approval.
  • Answer phones, take clerical messages, and route client inquiries to the appropriate licensed professional.
  • Retrieve public property records, tax cards, zoning maps, and municipal documents.
  • Order title searches, survey reports, and lien searches requested by the supervising broker.
All activities subject to broker supervision, Florida Statutes Chapter 475, and FREC guidelines. VIEW FREC GUIDANCE (PDF)
